Read-only survey, 9 Nov 2026. Repos: `RhythmWarfare/` (RW, Godot 4.5.1, landscape phone rhythm game) and `DuelMasterBattle/` (DMB, Godot 4.5, portrait wizard-sim). Paths are relative to `C:\Users\joesa\Documents\Cursor\UnityBedsit-refs\` unless shown as `origin/<branch>:`.

## 0. Headline findings

1. **RW already has a bedsit** with the exact brief (4 fixed compass views, 90 degree turns, left/right/bottom/top regions, fixed facing-to-theme map, relaxed inspection). It is built, tested headless, and merged into `origin/main`. Nobody has played it on a phone. Treat it as the design source of truth for room layout and naming.
2. All three requested RW branches (`feature/doom-layout-worlds`, `feature/bedsit-raves`, `feature/run-on-25d`) are **already merged into `origin/main`** (`git diff origin/main...origin/feature/<b>` is empty). Read `main`, not the branches. `docs/PICKUP.md` (5 Nov) predates the bedsit and Doom work and does not mention it; `docs/bedsit/LAUNCH.md` and `docs/doom/LAUNCH.md` (8-9 Nov) are the current entry points for those.
3. DMB has **no first-person, 2.5D, bedsit or mirror content**. Its value is: the character animation/dialogue-bus design (`feature/character-sprites-animation`), the dialogue JSON contract, mobile gesture/touch-size rules, and the Android build flow. `feature/god-world` is a top-down god game (Pandemic-style board); only its camera/gesture arbitration is reusable.
4. Neither repo has a real mirror. RW's mirror is a flat wall decal (`w_mirror`). `MirrorReflectionController` has no Godot precedent; it is new work.
5. The word "window" in RW means the **timed choice window** (beats 48-64 of a 64-beat scene), not a physical window. Avoid importing that term. A physical window is the `win_frame`/`win_glass` decals on the north wall.

## 1. RW: the existing bedsit (keep Unity consistent with this)

Sources: `RhythmWarfare/docs/bedsit/LAUNCH.md` (the doc), `content/story/maps/bedsit.json` (geometry), `content/bedsit/themes.json` and `routes.json`, `content/paths/bedsit/run.json` + `hotspots/bedsit.json`, `src/bedsit/bedsit_nav.gd` + `bedsit_room.gd`, `tests/test_bedsit*.gd`, `docs/briefs/BEDSIT_RAVES_BRIEF.md` (owner's words), `docs/shots/bedsit/bedsit_contact.jpg` (4 facings at 16:9 and 20:9).

### Room geometry
- One sector, **4 m (x) by 5 m (z), 2.5 m high**, eye 1.6 m, camera at the exact centre (0,0), **one point, four yaws**. Wall pictures are decals on their own wall, so it is one consistent room from every facing; small props are billboards. 160 texels/m.
- Walls are 2.0 m (E, W) and 2.5 m (N, S) from the camera. At eye 1.6 m and level gaze the 52 degree vertical FOV shows 0.63-2.57 m up the wall: **floor, rug, mattress, desk legs are out of frame**. RW's own review says a ~10 degree downward pitch would fix it (not done). Worth deciding early in Unity (FOV / pitch / camera height).
- Axes: RW +x = east, +z = south, -z = north (camera faces -z at yaw 0). RW yaw is **positive = turn left**: N 0, E -90, S 180, W 90. **Unity yaw is positive = turn right**, so use N 0, E 90, S 180, W 270 and do not copy the RW numbers.

### Which wall has what (camera at centre looking out)

|---|---|---|---|
| **N** (start) | Space | window frame + glass (centre, 1.45 m tall, bottom at 0.78 m), radiator below, two posters flanking, table lamp right | model rocket on sill, star mobile (sways), cold violet moon light on boards |
| **E** | Dinosaurs | desk with decks/mixer/monitors (centre), coat hooks left, poster right, headphones on desk, fern | jungle poster whose middle is a real glowing opening, plastic dino on desk, green light |

Floor: oak boards, kilim rug, record, socks, slippers, laundry, headphone lead (mostly never seen, see above). Lighting: warm room bulb + a coloured pool per threshold.

So a **mirror already lives on the west wall**, beside the bed and door. Keep it there. The bedsit's door is also the "way out" (west facing, middle of the door); top of that same door is the Transport threshold.

### Facing / input model (`src/bedsit/bedsit_nav.gd`, `bedsit_room.gd`, `themes.json` `regions`)
- Fixed mapping: **N Space, E Dinos, S Magic, W Transport** (validator refuses another). Start N. Turns wrap; four lefts = north.
- Screen regions as fractions of the stage area: left x 0-0.22 full height = turn left; right x 0.78-1 = turn right; bottom x 0.25-0.75, y 0.72-1 = stay; top x 0.25-0.75, y 0-0.42 = "leave through the facing's threshold". Middle gap (y 0.42-0.72) = the room's own hotspots (the way out). Regions are screen-space and never overlap the rhythm pads. Desktop: arrow keys = same four regions.
- Turn = camera yaws on the spot over 1 bar then **holds fully still** (no drift/glance/stroll: lead ruling 35a, "more moments with the camera relatively stationary"). Facing is written to the save at once (`bedsit_facing`: "N"|"E"|"S"|"W"); coming home cuts to the saved facing.
- Relaxed: the bedsit node has `relaxed: true`; missed beats cost nothing and the scene never forces a departure.

### Naming conventions to reuse
`bedsit_n/e/s/w` (viewpoint waypoints, same point, different yaw) and `threshold_n/e/s/w` (where you stand when leaving); hotspot ids `bedsit.threshold_<f>`, `bedsit.way_out`, `bedsit.dev_exit`; theme ids `space|magic|dinos|transport`; scene ids `space_rave`, `dino_rave`, `magic_rave`, `transport_rave` (the contract alias is `rave.<theme>`); flags `bedsit_facing`, `bedsit_way_out`, `rave.<theme>.bass_fixed|cloakroom_done|bar_done`; asset ids `wall_paper_n..w`, `thing_<x>`, `poster_0..4`, `door`, `door_light`, `mirror`, etc. (`assets/story/bedsit/manifest.json`).

### Doom-layout rules (relevant to "Doom-style" authoring)
Sources: `docs/doom/CONVERSION.md`, `docs/doom/LAUNCH.md`, `docs/briefs/DOOM_WORLDS_BRIEF.md`, `docs/art/STYLE_GUIDE.md`, `CAMERA_CLEARANCE.md`, `WALL_DECALS.md`, `docs/engine/GEOGRAPHY_WORLDS.md`.
- **Sectors are convex polygons with floor/ceiling heights; walls are edges; portals join sectors.** Maps are metres, 32 Doom units = 1 m, eye 1.6 m, Doom "north" = -z. Doom maps were converted into this format (approximate, traced; the supplied WAD had no geometry). Stable ids: `<sector>_<n>`.
- Wall-mounted things are **decals** (flat on a wall, 6 mm off it); free-standing things are **billboards** (face the camera). A billboard's `y` is its centre, a decal's `y` is its bottom edge. A decal may not float more than 0.35 m from a solid wall. (`WALL_DECALS.md`)
- Camera clearance rule: no wall nearer than 2.5 m filling more than half the picture (bedsit E/W at 2.0 m would trip it; RW exempts the bedsit, hard 0.6 m limit holds).
- Look: dark rooms, lifted coloured shadows, local light pools (every room needs a pool 2x brighter than its surroundings), never pure black, no pure white, no black outlines, nearest-filter pixels at 360 lines (`STYLE_GUIDE.md`). Exposure rule per 16:9 shot: median luminance 0.25-0.38 (Doom set) / 0.11-0.24 (style guide), contrast p95-p5 >= 0.40.
- The "four-area choice concept" in Doom scenes (bottom = stay, top/left/right = three destinations as **characters/guide things in the world**) is the same shape as the bedsit's four regions but done through world objects (hotspots), not screen regions (`CONTRACTS.md` sections 5 and 9).

## 2. First-person camera navigation -> `FirstPersonViewController`
- **Rail camera on waypoints, not free movement**: the camera only moves via named waypoints (`player_move {to, bars, style}`, `player_look {yaw|at, bars}`) from authored data (`docs/STORY_25D_PLAN.md` 4.1, `CONTRACTS.md` 3). Route = waypoint chain; hold after each leg. Maps to a Unity controller that eases yaw between four serialized yaws and exposes `Facing` plus `Turn(dir)`.
- **Held viewpoint** (`CONTRACTS.md` 3, ruling 35a): at a choice viewpoint the camera is *still*, with no bob, glance, or head drift, so touch targets do not move under the finger. Optional authored `pitch` (+-35 degrees, negative looks down) held through the interaction (`wip/engine-hold-pitch`, `tests/test_choice_pitch.gd`). Adopt both: 90 degree turn tween then hard hold, optional per-facing pitch.
- Turn timing in RW is musical (1 bar). In Unity use a fixed duration (e.g. 0.35-0.6 s, ease-in-out); no musical clock needed unless the song drives it.
- Eye follows floor height with a ramp at portals (`story_player.gd`); irrelevant for one flat room, relevant if the POC later has steps.
- Rejecting input mid-turn: RW refuses a turn after a threshold pick is "locked" (`BedsitRoom.region_tapped` "refused"). A turn withdraws a pending selection. Copy: a turn cancels any selected target; ignore turn input while a transition is committed.

## 3. Doom-style 2.5D environments -> `WorldSceneController`
- Engine split (RW): `StoryMap` (sectors/walls/things/waypoints JSON) -> `StoryWorld` (3D mesh) -> `StoryStage`/low-res pass (renders to 360 lines, upscales nearest) -> `StoryPlayer` (camera). Plain Unity equivalent: a low-res RenderTexture + point filter + billboard sprites + decal quads; one scene per room, data-driven. See `docs/STORY_25D_PLAN.md` section 2 (decision: real 3D, not a shader raycaster).
- **Scene = one map + one run node**. `WorldSceneController` should own: load room data, place decals/billboards, expose waypoints, hold the current facing, ask `PlayerTransitionController` for a cut. RW's `RunPlay` plays this role; do not copy its size (the Unity POC needs a fraction).
- **Dynamic things** (`docs/engine/WORLD_THINGS.md`): props that change by frame/position at runtime are `{id, sprite, at, sector, size, frame, y, anchor}` sheets (rows = states, columns = frames), stage draws them; the activity only declares state. Matches a Unity `SpriteRenderer` swap driven by `InteractionTarget` state. Also `piece_on/piece_off/piece_pulse` toggles a lit map piece (a lamp, a shelf glass).
- Lights are per-sector song-time light x lamps; billboard "emissive" marks self-lit pixels (windows, screens). Use per-sector ambient + point lights; mark emissive sprites.
- Weather/rain, fog (`fog.color`, density), sky strip are per-map data (`docs/engine/WEATHER.md`). Bedsit map has fog density 0.

## 4. Four-direction interaction -> `InteractionTarget` / `InteractionController` / `InputRouter`
- **Exactly one tap owner** (`CONTRACTS.md` 5, `ContractShapes.tap_owner`): `hud | pads | activity_controls | hotspots | world_regions | activity_view`. `InputRouter` should do the same: one function maps (screen point, current mode) to one owner. The bedsit's `world_regions` owner and one `dispatch_tap` (`RunPlay.dispatch_tap`, H5.5) is the exact precedent.
- **Targets without drawn rings** (`CONTRACTS.md` 9, ruling 35b): state is shown by the art itself (door lights/opens, figure beckons, lamp colour, floor glow), never by a uniform ring/arrow. States: `idle | available | selected | committed | declined | cost`; touch target = the thing's *projected art bounds* grown by <= 24 px and widened to >= 88 px (at 1280x720); no invisible hitboxes (`INTERACTION_DESIGN.md` 3.6); response within 0.1 s. This is a strong template for `InteractionTarget` (state machine + per-state sprite frames + motion + light) and for how `InteractionController` decides what is hit (projected renderer bounds, not colliders).
- **Selection vs commit**: a tap selects at once (within 0.1 s), commit happens at a boundary (in RW the next block; in Unity: end of the selection hold or confirm tap). A second tap elsewhere moves the selection. Facing change withdraws it.
- **Hotspot data** (`content/paths/bedsit/hotspots/bedsit.json`): `{id, anchor: {waypoint|thing|pos|actor}, kind: exit|object|person, radius_m, min_px: 88, available: {flags_all, flags_none}, on_success: {route}, show: {archetype: door|person|object|figure|custom}}`. Good schema reference for `InteractionTarget` serialisation; gating by flags (`bedsit_way_out`).
- **Choice archetypes** (`docs/CHOICE_CLARITY.md` section 2): person (beckon/plead/nod), door (open green lamp / shut red lamp), object (bobs), feature (recoils), figure. Use shape *and* light, never colour alone.
- Only the current facing's threshold is "on show"/hittable (`BedsitRoom.shows`); others are behind the camera. Do the same: enable only targets within the facing's frustum.
- Heralds (`docs/art/HERALDS.md`): a character stands at an entrance and gestures toward it; a sign over the door; a pool of coloured light on the floor. Direct analogue for NPCs "seen through a door/window": a person at a doorway looking at the camera and beckoning.

## 5. Character animation -> `SimCharacterActor` / `SimAnimationLibrary`
Two precedents, different tech:
- **RW (pixel sprite sheets for first person)**: `tools/story/art/people.py` bakes 3-view (front/side/back) sheets at LODs 1/2/4; rows = acts, columns = frames; **14 faces** `neutral happy cheer sad fear anger plea doubt relief awe grief laugh resolve shame`; acts such as idle look cheer clap hands_up beckon point nod plead kneel cover_face stare shake_head; activity states `waiting happy impatient frown beckon deal raise_glass` with **fallback chains** (`StoryCrowd.ACTIVITY_STATES`, `CROWD_FALLBACKS` in `src/story/story_crowd.gd`: ask for state X, else first available candidate in the sheet). Backed by `docs/FACE_POLISH.md`, `docs/art/STYLE_GUIDE.md` section 9 (head 1/6.5-1/7 of height, 3-step shading, light from upper left, no two people share `look_key`). **Directly reusable idea: `SimAnimationLibrary.Resolve(state)` with fallback lists so a missing clip never breaks.**
- **DMB `origin/feature/character-sprites-animation`** (`docs/CHARACTER_SPRITES_AND_ANIMATION.md`, `godot_project/client/characters/*.gd`):
  - `character_anim.gd` (`DmbCharAnim`): one state machine `IDLE WALK WORK TALK CAST CELEBRATE SURPRISE HURT DYING DEAD REVIVING` with **strict priority** DEAD > REVIVE > HURT > reaction > TALK > WALK/WORK > IDLE (lower never interrupts higher; one-shot reactions use a hold timer; walk has 0.12 s hysteresis so one stalled frame does not flicker). Blink and idle phase come from a per-character phase seed. Maps to `SimCharacterActor` state + Animator parameters.
  - `character_bus.gd` (`DmbCharBus`): presentation events keyed by stable character id (`speak(id, seconds)`, `speak_text(id, text)` uses ~22 chars/s min 1.2 s, `react(id, kind)`, `mark_dead`). Nothing flows back to sim. Good model for a dialogue system that makes the speaker's mouth animate without coupling.
  - `character_look.gd`/`character_appearance.gd`: a look is a **pure function of a stable id** (FNV-1a of id), so every client/save/reload agrees. Use this for NPC variety in the bedsit POC.
  - `tools/character_sprites/` (Python rig: clips, garments, hats, hair, face, 72 outfits, layer sheets tintable). Procedural, deterministic, 81 pytest tests. Too heavy to copy; the *layered, tintable, per-id-seeded* idea is the takeaway.
  - Facing helpers: `facing_from_vector`, `facing_from_compass` (n/e/s/w -> up/right/down/left). Same N/E/S/W convention as the bedsit.
- Both: **dialogue portraits/faces carry emotion**; RW deliberately writes no text during play (D3 silent HUD, `RW_DEV_TEXT=1` brings it back). Consider NPCs acting, not talking, in the POC.

## 6. Dialogue and scripted actions -> `SimCharacterActor` + `WorldSceneController`
- **RW acting contract** (`docs/THREE_PATHS_AND_FACES_PLAN.md` 2.3): data, not code, directs a person. (a) `acting` on a hotspot: per interaction state (`available/selected/committed/success/failure`) `{act, face, look_at: camera|actor|thing|waypoint, beats, then}`. (b) `cast` on a scene: timed story beats `[{actor, at_beat, act, face, look_at, beats, if_energy_below, if_flags_all}]` that fire whether or not the player acts. (c) cinematic `steps: [{move: walk, to, bars, style, look_at}]`. This is the best conceptual template for scripted actions; replace "beats" with seconds.
- **DMB dialogue contract**: nodes are JSON `{schema_version, id, scope, speaker_role, era_id, quest_id, stage, text, fallback, condition:{op, args}, choices:[{id,label,effects:[{kind,effect_id,target_id}], next}]}` (example: `DuelMasterBattle/godot_project/content/source/dialogue/boulder_quest.json`); `fallback` is a no-text description for silence. Typed effects with receipts only, conditions are read from state at the moment. `client/ui/dialogue.gd` (`DmbDialoguePresenter`): `show_ambient` (no pause) vs `open_formal` (acquires a pause token; walk-away gives no reward). `docs/WIZARD_CONVERSATIONS_AND_QUESTS.md`: a person being talked to is **held in place** while the world runs on; a conversation ends on acknowledge/leave/walk away/another conversation/death/idle timeout/load; only an explicit `"ends": true` choice ends it. Useful for a Unity dialogue stub: ambient lines vs formal conversation, explicit end conditions, speaker hold.
- Do not import DMB's heavy generator (`tools/dialogue_generation`, workbook xlsx) for the POC.

## 7. Scene transitions -> `PlayerTransitionController`
- RW contract 1 (`CONTRACTS.md` section 1, `docs/engine/GEOGRAPHY_WORLDS.md`): **world transition** (to another map) = walk to a threshold waypoint (`threshold_<facing>`), a "passage frame" shuts over the swap on the downbeat, the new scene opens at an arrival pose (`rail.arrive`) and walks home, the frame reopens; **passage** (inside one map) = a route of waypoints, no swap. `SceneEntry {scene_id, theme, arrival_pose, entered_from, transition}` and `SceneExit {route|home, transition, threshold}`. "Home" returns to the bedsit facing the threshold you left by (`bedsit_facing`).
- Pace rule: walk-outs at <= 3 m/s over 2-8 bars (`RunRail.walk_off_bars`); a leg needing more is a scene authoring fault (checked by `tools/story/check_rail_paths.gd`). Never a cut without a doorway moment; surreal thresholds are authored in the room (jungle poster opening, wardrobe slot).
- Commit semantics: selecting a threshold does nothing until the boundary; the walk starts from the tap in Doom "geography" worlds (ruling 39) but at the lock in the bedsit (ruling 37, next block boundary). For Unity: pick one (recommended: tap selects + highlights, tap again or hold commits; walk to threshold; fade/frame; swap; arrive; reopen).
- The rave is never left by rhythm failure; bedsit has no `on_miss`.

## 8. Mirror -> `MirrorReflectionController`
No precedent in code. Only RW's flat `mirror` decal on the W wall (`mirror.png`, 1.45 m tall, bottom 0.35 m). In Unity this needs a real second camera/RenderTexture or a planar reflection; the player's own figure is never drawn in RW (D7, "the player is never seen; the camera is the hero"), so the mirror is a design departure. Keep its W-wall position (left of the door, south end) for consistency.

## 9. Mobile input -> `InputRouter`
- RW: landscape, 16:9 and 20:9 both shot and tested; touch regions are **screen fractions** of the stage area (above the pad strip), min target **88 px at 1280x720** (~48 dp; open decision whether to raise to 96, `LEAD_RULINGS.md` 3). Multi-touch for pads (`INTERACTION_DESIGN.md` 7.2). Desktop arrow keys mirror the regions. Dev number keys 1-8 pick any choice, off in the bedsit unless `RW_DEV_KEYS=1`. Tap targets projected from art bounds. `src/input/lane_input.gd` handles touches/section taps/keys.
- DMB god-world `god_camera.gd` (`origin/feature/god-world`): gesture arbitration, one finger = pan once past 8 dp (converted via `DisplayServer.screen_get_dpi()/160`), short press without drag = tap, two fingers = pinch around midpoint, a drag or pinch never taps on release, emulated mouse events ignored in favour of ScreenTouch/ScreenDrag. 250 ms pinch block, 600 ms max tap. Useful rules for `InputRouter` (tap vs swipe disambiguation, DPI-based thresholds; a swipe-to-turn alternative to the edge regions).
- DMB UI: every button >= 48 px (`NARR_TOUCH_MIN`), body text >= 16 physical px, tested at five window sizes (`run_hybrid_ui.gd`); haptics via `Input.vibrate_handheld(20/40/30)` (`client/scripts/playability_haptics.gd`) with a settings toggle. Taps within labels: tap thing -> small label, tap label -> details, tap empty ground dismisses.
- Portrait vs landscape: RW landscape, DMB portrait 720x1280. The bedsit is landscape.
- **User's 3 inputs vs RW's 4 regions**: RW has left / right / bottom=stay / top=leave. Your POC's left / right / top-centre interact fits as RW left, right, top; bottom (stay) is not required if "do nothing" is simply not tapping. Keep a visible middle region for room hotspots only if needed.
